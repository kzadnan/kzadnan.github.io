Research focus

I study how heat crosses solid–solid interfaces in wide- and ultrawide-bandgap electronics. The question I am trying to settle is whether thermal boundary conductance is a property of one junction, or of the stack around it. I train machine-learning interatomic potentials on density-functional forces and use them to run nonequilibrium molecular dynamics at lengths direct DFT cannot reach. Four first-author papers in Physical Review B, Physical Review Applied, Applied Physics Letters, and the International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer.

Selected results

  • A nearby interface is not a spectator. In Si/Ge, adding one raised thermal boundary conductance from 400 to 700 MW m−2 K−1. A 30 nm Si film between Ge increased apparent thermal conductivity from 50 to 280 W m−1 K−1, above bulk Si, by filtering which phonon modes carry the heat (Phys. Rev. B 2024).
  • The same idea holds in GaN/AlN: a standalone interface is about 600 MW m−2 K−1 after quantum correction; a second nearby interface can raise it to about 1000 by ballistic filtering, not by forming superlattice minibands (Phys. Rev. B 2025).
  • A DFT-trained potential was required to match Si/diamond experiment (130 vs. ~140 MW m−2 K−1; Tersoff molecular dynamics gave 310). A 1 nm SiNx interlayer raised conductance to 275, and amorphous carbon to 350. In a silicon-on-diamond device model, lifting conductance from 50 to 350 cut the hot-spot temperature rise by tens of kelvin (Phys. Rev. Applied 2026).
  • An AlGaN interlayer between GaN and AlN lowers conductance across the alloy range; alloy scattering beats the usual phonon-bridge picture. A sigmoidal composition grade recovers it (Appl. Phys. Lett. 2026).
  • With U.S. Army DEVCOM, quantum-corrected metal/diamond conductances for Al, Mo, Zr, and Au are about 284, 93, 30, and 40 MW m−2 K−1. They track the metal Debye temperature more than elastic modulus (Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 2024).

Research experience

Graduate Research Assistant — Feng Research Group, University of Utah
2023 – present
  • Own the successive-interface calculations: design the cells, choose the spacer against the phonon mean free path, and separate real interface–interface interplay from finite-size artifacts. That protocol is what the later GaN/AlN, Si/diamond, and GaN/diamond studies are built on.
  • Train DeePMD and moment-tensor potentials on DFT forces for chemically abrupt, alloyed, and metal–diamond interfaces, then drive nonequilibrium molecular dynamics in cells direct first-principles dynamics cannot reach. DFT reference cells are typically 300–500 atoms.
  • Put classical molecular dynamics, the diffuse mismatch model, and experiment on one scale by implementing a quantum correction for mode occupation. Without it, room-temperature comparisons for stiff crystals such as diamond are not meaningful.
  • Carry atomistic conductance into a finite-element silicon-on-diamond device model so a change in interfacial resistance shows up as hot-spot temperature, not only as a materials number.
  • Collaborate with U.S. Army DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory on metal/diamond contacts (published) and with Sandia National Laboratories on GaN/diamond thermal boundary conductance (in progress).
