About

The scientific challenge

Heat in wide- and ultrawide-bandgap electronics often stalls at material junctions, not in the bulk crystals. Thermal boundary conductance (TBC) is the interfacial heat current per unit area per unit temperature drop. Most compact models treat TBC as an intrinsic property of one interface.

I test that assumption with computation: density-functional theory (DFT) labels forces, machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) carry those forces into large cells, and nonequilibrium molecular dynamics (NEMD) extracts TBC. The work is computational, not a thermal-measurement lab. Reported TBC values below follow the quantum-corrected NEMD numbers in the cited papers unless noted.

The practical target is stacks that appear in high-power RF and power devices: GaN/AlN, Si/diamond, metal/diamond, and GaN/diamond.

These interface numbers change device outcomes. In the Si/diamond study I coupled the atomistic TBC results to a finite-element silicon-on-diamond device model. Raising Si/diamond TBC from 50 to 350 MW m−2 K−1 lowers the hot-spot temperature rise from about 135 K to about 105 K for a 1 µm Si layer on 50 µm diamond. Once the Si layer drops below roughly 0.7 µm and spreading resistance dominates, the same TBC increase cuts the peak rise from about 165 K to about 95 K. Because roughly 10 K of junction-temperature reduction can extend device lifetime by about 50%, interfacial engineering is a reliability lever for power and RF electronics rather than a second-order correction.

Research

Three themes

One thesis, three questions: how a neighboring interface changes TBC, when an interlayer helps or hurts, and when a DFT-trained potential is required to get TBC right.

01

Successive interfaces

TBC of one junction depends on what sits next door until the gap exceeds a phonon mean-free-path scale. In Si/Ge NEMD, adding a nearby interface raised TBC from 400 to 700 MW m−2 K−1. A 30 nm Si film between Ge slabs increased apparent thermal conductivity from 50 to 280 W m−1 K−1 by admitting low-frequency heat-carrying modes. In GaN/AlN, a standalone interface is about 600 MW m−2 K−1 at 300 K after quantum correction; a second nearby interface can raise that to about 1000 MW m−2 K−1 by ballistic filtering of existing bulk modes, not superlattice minibands.

My contribution. First-author demonstration of the effect in Si/Ge, and the methodological framing the group’s later successive-interface work builds on: how to set up, size, and interpret multi-interface NEMD cells so that genuine interface–interface interplay is separable from finite-size artifacts. That framework sets the agenda for the rest of the thesis.

Interplay schematic · Phys. Rev. B 109, 245302 (2024) · Phys. Rev. B 112, 235308 (2025)

02

Interlayers and alloys

A nanometer film can bridge mismatched vibrations or add scattering. For Si/diamond, quantum-corrected NEMD with an MLIP gives a bare TBC of 130 MW m−2 K−1 (near experiment ~140), versus 310 from Tersoff MD. A 1 nm SiNx interlayer raises TBC to 275; amorphous carbon to 350, by supplying intermediate phonon modes. For GaN/AlN, an Al1−xGaxN interlayer reduces TBC over the alloy range — alloy scattering outweighs the usual phonon-bridge picture. A sigmoidal Al-composition grade recovers conductance relative to an abrupt or linear profile.

Phys. Rev. Applied 25, 044013 (2026) · Appl. Phys. Lett. 128, 122201 (2026)

03

DFT-trained interface MLIPs

Empirical potentials often misrepresent interfacial bonding. DeePMD and MTP models trained on DFT forces are used to drive NEMD in cells too large for direct DFT. For relaxed Al, Mo, Zr, and Au on diamond, quantum-corrected TBC values are about 284, 93, 30, and 40 MW m−2 K−1 — work carried out with U.S. Army DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory. Those resistances track the metal phonon specific heat and Debye temperature more closely than elastic modulus. Acoustic and diffuse mismatch models miss interfacial structure and inelastic transmission even with full-band DFT phonons. GaN/diamond TBC with Sandia National Laboratories is in progress.

Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 235, 126227 (2024) · Research software

Publications

Peer-reviewed papers

Reverse chronological order. First-author work is marked. Full records: Google Scholar. Citation counts are omitted here so they do not go stale; use Scholar for current numbers.

  1. Applied Physics Letters 2026 First author

    Significant impact of Al1−xGaxN interlayer on GaN/AlN thermal boundary conductance

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Hao Zhou, and Tianli Feng

    Appl. Phys. Lett. 128, 122201 (2026).

    AlGaN between GaN and AlN reduces TBC over the full alloy range; a sigmoidal composition grade recovers conductance relative to abrupt or linear profiles.

    My contribution. Compositional disorder in AlGaN makes the potential-energy surface far more complex than a binary interface, since every alloy composition is effectively a different material. Instead of trying to cover the whole alloy space, I trained the potential only on the structures relevant to the compositions and interface geometries actually simulated, which kept the fit accurate at tractable cost, then ran the NEMD showing that alloy scattering outweighs the usual phonon-bridge benefit.

    DOI for AlGaN interlayer paper arXiv:2601.12151
  2. Physical Review Applied 2026 First author

    Si/diamond thermal boundary conductance enhanced by SiNx and amorphous carbon interlayers

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Tanvirul Abedien, and Tianli Feng

    Phys. Rev. Applied 25, 044013 (2026).

    After quantum correction, bare Si/diamond TBC is 130 MW m−2 K−1; 1 nm SiNx gives 275, amorphous carbon 350.

    My contribution. Si and diamond are badly lattice-mismatched, and the usual expectation is that the resulting interfacial strain destroys any clean phonon description. I showed that it does not: the junction carries high strain, yet mode-resolved transport stays interpretable, so interlayer choice can still be reasoned about in phonon terms instead of being treated as a disordered black box. I also built the finite-element silicon-on-diamond device model that converts these TBC values into hot-spot temperature.

    DOI for Si/diamond interlayer paper Physical Review Applied article
  3. Physical Review B 2025

    Thermal boundary conductance in standalone and non-standalone GaN/AlN heterostructures predicted using machine-learning interatomic potentials

    Hao Zhou, Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Wyatt Allen Jones, and Tianli Feng

    Phys. Rev. B 112, 235308 (2025).

    Standalone GaN/AlN TBC is about 600 MW m−2 K−1 at 300 K after quantum correction; a nearby second interface can raise it to about 1000 by ballistic filtering.

    My contribution. I built a mismatch cross-section analysis for the unstrained GaN/AlN interface, the counterpart of the treatment developed for Si/Ge, so the two systems could be compared on the same basis. The governing difficulty here is cell length: resolving the interplay length over which a second interface still changes TBC requires simulation cells long enough to reach the ballistic-to-diffusive crossover, which is where most of the computational cost sits.

    DOI for GaN/AlN standalone paper arXiv:2503.05084
  4. International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer 2024 First author

    Thermal boundary conductance of metal–diamond interfaces predicted by machine learning interatomic potentials

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Mahesh R. Neupane, and Tianli Feng

    Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 235, 126227 (2024). With U.S. Army DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory.

    Quantum-corrected TBC for Al, Mo, Zr, and Au on diamond is about 284, 93, 30, and 40 MW m−2 K−1; TBC tracks metal Debye temperature more than elastic modulus.

    My contribution. I implemented and quantified the quantum correction factor for the classical diffuse mismatch model (DMM). Classical MD and classical DMM both assume every vibrational mode is fully excited, which is wrong at room temperature for stiff materials such as diamond. Correcting for the actual mode occupation put MD, DMM, and experiment on the same footing, which is what makes the comparison across Al, Mo, Zr, and Au meaningful rather than an artifact of the classical limit.

    DOI for metal–diamond paper arXiv:2404.15465

    Thermal boundary conductance and thermal conductivity strongly depend on nearby environment

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan and Tianli Feng

    A nearby interface after Si/Ge raised TBC from 400 to 700 MW m−2 K−1. A 30 nm Si layer between Ge increased apparent thermal conductivity from 50 to 280 W m−1 K−1.

Methods and software

How the calculations are done

Typical loop: build an interfacial supercell, label forces with DFT (VASP or Quantum ESPRESSO), train DeePMD or MTP, run NEMD in LAMMPS or GPUMD on multi-GPU clusters, and extract TBC from the steady temperature drop. Phonopy is used for bulk phonon checks. This is not a first-principles TBC calculation; the MLIP is trained to DFT forces and then used at MD length scales.

MLIPs for interfaced materials

DeePMD and MTP potentials for heterogeneous interfaces (including Si/diamond and Au/diamond), packaged for LAMMPS TBC runs.

JAX-MLIP

Harmonic-constrained potentials in JAX that learn force-constant structure from displacement data.

Autoencoder DMD

Mode extraction and reconstruction of ab initio molecular-dynamics trajectories with dynamic mode decomposition.

Coursework codes

Separate from the thesis: a radiation heat-transfer solver and heat-exchanger design models (Python/EES).
