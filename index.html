About

Computational heat transfer at the nanoscale

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Utah, working in the Feng research group. My work asks a practical question with a phonon-physics answer: how does heat leave a device when the bottleneck is not the bulk crystal, but the interfaces between materials?

Most thermal models treat thermal boundary conductance (TBC) as a property of a single interface. Across Si/Ge, GaN/AlN, Si/diamond, and metal/diamond stacks, I have shown that TBC and even apparent thermal conductivity depend on the nearby environment — a second interface, an interlayer, or a composition grade a few nanometers away.

I train machine-learning interatomic potentials from density-functional theory, then run large-scale nonequilibrium molecular dynamics on HPC clusters to predict TBC at first-principles accuracy. The goal is electro-thermal design rules for wide- and ultrawide-bandgap electronics.

Research interests

Problems I am actively working on

These are the specific questions that organize my thesis, not a generic keyword list. Each line is a research thread with published or in-progress results.

01

Successive-interface phonon transport

TBC is not an intrinsic interface property. A nearby interface acts as a phonon filter: it changes which modes arrive at the original junction and can raise TBC by 1–1.5× (and more in multilayers). The effect vanishes in the diffusive limit, when the gap exceeds a critical separation set by the mean free path of the filtered modes. I am mapping this for Si/Ge and III-nitride stacks, including how a thin film’s apparent thermal conductivity can exceed its standalone value when neighbors select heat-carrying acoustic modes.

  • Phonon-mode filtering by a second interface
  • Critical separation distance ℓcs vs. mean free path
  • Standalone vs. non-standalone heterostructures
02

MLIPs for heterogeneous interfaces

Empirical potentials (Tersoff, EAM, and related forms) systematically misrepresent interfacial bonding and over- or under-predict TBC. I train DeePMD and MTP models on DFT forces for chemically abrupt and mixed interfaces, then drive NEMD at lengths inaccessible to direct DFT. The target is >95% force accuracy on held-out interfacial configurations, with quantum corrections applied to the classical TBC.

  • DFT → active-learning → LAMMPS/GPUMD pipelines
  • Metal–C, Si–C, and III-nitride alloy potentials
  • Validation against phonon dispersion and experiment
03

Interlayer and composition engineering

Interlayers can help or hurt, and the sign is chemistry-dependent. At Si/diamond, 1 nm of SiNx roughly doubles TBC (130 → 275 MW m−2 K−1); amorphous carbon raises it further to ~350 MW m−2 K−1 by supplying intermediate phonon modes. At GaN/AlN, an Al1−xGaxN interlayer degrades TBC through alloy scattering — contrary to the usual “phonon bridge” intuition — while a sigmoidal Al-composition grade can recover conductance relative to an abrupt or linear profile.

  • Phonon-bridge interlayers (SiNx, a-C)
  • AlGaN alloy scattering vs. spectral bridging
  • Sigmoidal vs. linear composition grades
04

Thermal management of WBG / UWBG devices

GaN, AlN, and diamond are the materials of high-power RF, power conversion, and AI accelerators. I quantify TBC for the contacts that actually appear in stacks: GaN/AlN, GaN/SiC, GaN/diamond, Si/diamond, and metal (Al, Zr, Mo, Au)/diamond. For metal–diamond, quantum-corrected TBC tracks the metal’s phonon specific heat and Debye temperature more closely than elastic modulus — and the equivalent thermal-resistance thicknesses are large enough to matter in device models.

  • Diamond as a heat spreader; metal–diamond TBC
  • GaN HEMT-relevant III-nitride junctions
  • Finite-element coupling of TBC to peak chip temperature
05

Phonons, IFCs, and scientific machine learning

Beyond TBC numbers, I care about the operators that generate them: second- and higher-order interatomic force constants, spectral heat flux, and reduced-order models of AIMD trajectories. Current tools include autoencoder–DMD reconstructions of phonon motion, harmonic MLIPs in JAX, and grey-model mean-free-path estimates for amorphous layers.

  • IFC2/IFC3 from finite-displacement DFT
  • Autoencoder dynamic mode decomposition
  • Amorphous-layer phonon MFP models
06

High-throughput and agentic simulation workflows

Interface geometry, orientation, and interlayer thickness form a combinatorial space. I am building HPC workflows (SLURM, multi-GPU) that connect structure generation, DFT labeling, MLIP training, and NEMD TBC extraction — including self-healing job recovery and active learning when model deviation spikes. Longer term, I am exploring quantum-algorithm mappings of lattice-dynamical linear systems as a complementary route to classical IFC evaluation.

  • HPC molecular dynamics and DFT at scale
  • Active learning for interfacial MLIPs
  • Quantum / hybrid algorithms for lattice dynamics

Publications

Peer-reviewed papers

First-author work is marked. Citations from Google Scholar (August 2026). Full records: Scholar profile.

  1. Applied Physics Letters 2026 First author

    Significant impact of Al1−xGaxN interlayer on GaN/AlN thermal boundary conductance

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Hao Zhou, and Tianli Feng

    Appl. Phys. Lett. 128, 122201 (2026).

    AlN/GaN stacks in RF and power devices often contain an AlGaN interlayer, either from growth or to form a 2DEG. Using deep-learning potentials trained on DFT, we show that this interlayer reduces GaN/AlN TBC over the full alloy range — alloy scattering beats the usual phonon-bridge picture. A sigmoidal Al-composition grade recovers TBC relative to abrupt or linear profiles, giving a concrete electro-thermal design rule.

    DOI arXiv
  2. Physical Review Applied 2026 First author

    Si/diamond thermal boundary conductance enhanced by SiNx and amorphous carbon interlayers

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Tanvirul Abedien, and Tianli Feng

    Phys. Rev. Applied 25, 044013 (2026).

    MLIP-driven NEMD, after quantum correction, gives a bare Si/diamond TBC of 130 MW m−2 K−1 — matching experiment (~140) and well below Tersoff MD (310) or a first-principles DMM (205). A 1 nm SiNx interlayer approximately doubles TBC to 275; amorphous carbon raises it to 350 by bridging intermediate phonon modes. Device-level FEM shows a measurable drop in peak chip temperature.

    DOI Journal
  3. Physical Review B 2025

    Thermal boundary conductance in standalone and nonstandalone GaN/AlN heterostructures predicted using machine-learning interatomic potentials

    Hao Zhou, Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Wyatt Allen Jones, and Tianli Feng

    Phys. Rev. B 112, 235308 (2025).

    Standalone GaN/AlN TBC is ~600 MW m−2 K−1 at 300 K after quantum correction, revising the 400–2000 range from empirical potentials. Bringing a second interface nearby raises the original TBC to ~1000, and additional nearby interfaces push it above 1150. The enhancement is ballistic filtering of existing bulk modes, not superlattice minibands. We define a critical separation distance set by the mean free path of the filtered phonons.

    DOI arXiv Journal
  4. Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 2024 First author 24 citations

    Thermal boundary conductance of metal–diamond interfaces predicted by machine learning interatomic potentials

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan, Mahesh R. Neupane, and Tianli Feng

    Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 235, 126227 (2024).

    We built MLIPs for Al, Mo, Zr, and Au on diamond and predicted quantum-corrected TBCs of ~284, 93, 30, and 40 MW m−2 K−1. Those resistances equal 0.83 μm of Al, 1.5 μm of Mo, 0.73 μm of Zr, and 7.9 μm of Au. AMM/DMM — even with full-band DFT phonons — miss interfacial structure and inelastic transmission. TBC correlates with the metal’s phonon specific heat and Debye temperature, not diamond’s, and more strongly with Debye temperature than with elastic modulus.

    DOI arXiv Journal
  5. Physical Review B 2024 First author 14 citations

    Thermal boundary conductance and thermal conductivity strongly depend on nearby environment

    Khalid Zobaid Adnan and Tianli Feng

    Phys. Rev. B 109, 245302 (2024).

    The opening paper of the successive-interface program. Adding a nearby interface after Si/Ge raises TBC from 400 to 700 MW m−2 K−1 because the second junction filters phonon modes. Sandwiching 30 nm of Si between Ge slabs raises its apparent thermal conductivity from 50 to 280 W m−1 K−1 — above bulk Si — by admitting low-frequency heat-carrying modes and rejecting optical ones. The effect disappears when the interface spacing exceeds the relevant mean free paths.

    DOI Journal
